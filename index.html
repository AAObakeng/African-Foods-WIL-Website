1. IT DEPARTMENT STRUCTURE AND REPORTING

Purpose: To explain the IT responsibilities and reporting structure across the company's four branches.

IT Manager

The IT Manager oversees IT operations, approves IT activities and reports major problems to Executive Management.

Network Administrator

The Network Administrator maintains routers, switches, network connections, IP addresses and network documentation.

IT Support Technician

The IT Support Technician assists employees with computers, printers, accounts and basic network problems.

Branch IT Contacts

Each branch in Durban, Gqeberha and Polokwane should have a nominated employee who reports local IT problems to the support team.

Employees

All employees must follow IT policies, protect company information and report IT problems.

Reporting Procedure

  1. Employees report IT problems to the designated service desk.
  2. The support team records the problem and assigns it to the correct person.
  3. Network and server problems are escalated to the Network Administrator.
  4. Major problems are reported to the IT Manager and Executive Management.
  5. The support team records the solution before closing the request.

2. IT SECURITY POLICY

Purpose: To protect company computers, accounts and information from unauthorised access, damage and theft.

Security Rules

  • Employees must use their own accounts and must not share passwords.
  • Passwords must be strong and difficult to guess.
  • Employees must lock their computers when leaving their desks.
  • Only approved software may be installed on company computers.
  • Employees must not open suspicious email attachments or links.
  • Company information must only be shared with authorised people.
  • Payroll and financial information must be protected.
  • Unknown USB devices must not be connected to company computers.
  • Lost devices and suspected security incidents must be reported immediately.
  • Employees must not disable antivirus software or other security controls.

Security Incident Procedure

  1. Stop interacting with suspicious messages or files.
  2. Report the incident to IT support or the IT Manager immediately.
  3. Explain which computer, branch or system is affected.
  4. IT staff investigate the incident and take steps to prevent further damage.
  5. Services are restored after the affected systems have been checked.

Policy Review

The IT Manager should review this policy annually and whenever a major security incident occurs.

3. ACCEPTABLE USE POLICY

Purpose: To explain how employees are expected to use company computers, internet access, email and business information.

Acceptable Use

  • Company computers must be used mainly for authorised business activities.
  • Employees must communicate professionally using company email.
  • Employees may only access information required for their work.
  • Company devices and information must be kept secure.
  • Work documents must be saved in approved storage locations.
  • IT problems must be reported to the support team.

Prohibited Use

  • Employees must not install unapproved software.
  • Company systems must not be used for illegal activities.
  • Employees must not access another person's account without permission.
  • Company information must not be copied to personal storage without authorisation.
  • Confidential customer, payroll and supplier information must not be shared with unauthorised people.
  • Employees must not use company systems to distribute malware or spam.

Monitoring and Enforcement

Company systems may be monitored for legitimate security and support purposes in accordance with applicable law and company rules. Misuse may result in restricted access or disciplinary action under company procedures.

4. IT ACQUISITION AND APPROVALS PROCEDURE

Purpose: To ensure that IT equipment and software are suitable for the company and approved before money is spent.

Acquisition Procedure

  1. The employee or department submits a request explaining what equipment or software is needed and why.
  2. The request includes the quantity, branch, estimated cost and required date.
  3. IT checks whether existing equipment can meet the requirement.
  4. The Network Administrator checks compatibility, security and technical requirements.
  5. The IT Manager reviews the recommendation and available budget.
  6. Executive Management or an authorised budget holder approves the purchase in writing.
  7. Procurement obtains quotations according to company purchasing rules.
  8. Equipment must not be ordered before approval is received.
  9. IT checks delivered equipment and records its details in the asset register.
  10. IT installs approved software, applies security updates and assigns the equipment to the correct employee.

Before Approval

  • Check compatibility with company computers and existing software.
  • Check software licences and security updates.
  • Check warranty and maintenance costs.
  • Confirm that the purchase is within the approved budget.

Records to Keep

The company should keep purchase requests, quotations, approvals, invoices, licence details and asset records.

5. IT SERVICE DESK / HELP DESK PROCEDURE

Purpose: To ensure IT problems are recorded, prioritised and resolved consistently across all company branches.

Information Employees Must Provide

  • Employee name and contact details.
  • Branch where the problem occurred.
  • Computer or system affected.
  • Description of the problem and any error message.
  • Time the problem started and how it affects work.

Employees must never include their passwords in support requests.

Help Desk Procedure

  1. The support team records the request and gives it a ticket number.
  2. The problem is classified as an incident, service request or security incident.
  3. The team determines the priority according to the impact on business operations.
  4. The employee receives the ticket number and relevant updates.
  5. The support team performs basic checks, such as checking power, cables and network connections.
  6. Serious network, server and security problems are escalated to the appropriate IT staff.
  7. All troubleshooting steps and actions are recorded.
  8. The employee confirms that the problem is resolved before the ticket is closed.

Suggested Support Priorities

Priority Example Suggested Response
Critical Central database unavailable or multiple branches affected. Within 15 minutes.
High An important department function is seriously affected. Within 1 business hour.
Normal One employee has a problem but can use a workaround. Within 1 business day.
Low Routine request or general question. Within 2 business days.

These are suggested response targets and must be approved by company management.

6. DISASTER RECOVERY AND BUSINESS CONTINUITY PLAN

Purpose: To guide the company when IT services are disrupted by server failure, malware, data loss, power failure, internet failure or physical damage.

Preparation

  • Maintain an inventory of computers, servers, routers, switches and software.
  • Keep three copies of important data on two different storage types, with one copy stored offsite.
  • Protect backups using encryption and restricted access.
  • Run backups regularly and test whether data can be restored.
  • Keep network diagrams, configuration backups and recovery instructions in a secure location.
  • Use UPS units to protect critical equipment from short power interruptions.
  • Keep contact details for the internet provider and technical support providers.

Proposed Recovery Targets

Recovery Time Objective (RTO): Aim to restore core database services within four hours.

Recovery Point Objective (RPO): Aim to limit data loss to no more than one hour.

These targets must be checked against the company's actual technology and backup capabilities.

Disaster Recovery Procedure

  1. Report the incident to the IT Manager immediately.
  2. Record the time, affected branch, systems and business impact.
  3. Protect people first and follow emergency procedures if there is physical danger.
  4. The IT Manager informs Executive Management if a major IT disaster is declared.
  5. Isolate affected systems when necessary to prevent further damage.
  6. Contact the relevant internet provider, backup provider or hardware support provider.
  7. Restore essential systems from a known-clean backup.
  8. Test data integrity, user access, branch connectivity and important business functions.
  9. Inform employees when services are restored.
  10. Record the recovery actions and review what can be improved.

Backup Testing

The company should test backup restoration at least twice a year and after major system changes. Each test should record the date, result, time taken and any problems discovered.
